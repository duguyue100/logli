I think many people underestimate the powerfulness of byte pair encoding (BPE). It's an great universal representation with a limited size of vocabulary. I've been thinking about a hypothesis which I will spend even more time on how to validate it, so it goes like this:

1. By this time, I think we can view autoregressive machines such as transformers as an online programming machine. It literally spits out program in the visual form of tokens.
2. And because of this, a limit-sized vocabulary becomes a powerful regularization without sacrificing generality.
3. But, the vocabulary is computed from an empirical distribution, which seemingly powerful, but may also embed many shortcomings. The classic argument would be: "Language is so imprecise, and you can't count on it to do precise stuff".
4. My core hypothesis is: the effect of using a vocabulary as a form of regularization is very under studied, and I think there is likely a more powerful learning machine if we don't constrain the learning machines to operate only in the space of the vocabulary. This would sacrifice the interpretability because from human's perspective, the machine will not speak human readable language anymore. But, this might actually unlock some very surprising intelligence behaviors.
5. My feeling is that researchers have agreed on some chosen hyperparameters such as vocabulary sizes for different models, but we haven't had the chance to really test the limit of this form of regularization and whether we have a better alternative, capability wise.
