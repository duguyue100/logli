I've always felt that humanoid robot is a long-term research objective than a pratical pursuit of robotic transformation of the society. Engineering solution to a practical problem is often not the same how a human would solve it by hands. The prime examples would be dishwasher and washing machine. As a human, we wouldn't and couldn't develop the same washing routine with our bare hands. Humans are constrained to do many things well, but not exceptional, at a reasonable scale. We are not building robots to move heavy things with their robot arms in a long distance, we got trucks (and self-driving trucks) for that matter.

At this moment of history, we need more dedicated robotic solutions and robot-friendly infrastructure built for a better, more efficient environment where robots can finally co-live with human without being a hassle. The whole reason that we have all the robot dogs, drones, and humanoids is because our infrastructure is human-friendly but not robot-friendly. Even if we finally got the right robot that works in every terrain, the chance is we still would need to undergo the infra changes anyway. The hype of current humanoid race is hence largely meaningless in my opinion.
